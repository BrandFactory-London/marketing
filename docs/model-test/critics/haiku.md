# Critic report: Migss Interiors drafts D1 to D6

Draft types: D1, D3 and D6 are cost guides ("How much does a bathroom renovation cost in Essex?"). D2, D4 and D5 are wet room comparison posts ("Wet room or shower tray: which suits your bathroom?").

Every draft has at least one hard-rule or facts-discipline breach, so all six are FAIL.

## D1
Verdict: FAIL
Scores: voice 7, usefulness 8, specificity 7
Issues:
- [rule of three] "A bigger room means more tiling, more materials and more labour hours." : three parallel "more" phrases used for rhythm.
- [rule of three] "we will go through your room, your plans and your budget" : parallel triad in the closing CTA.
- [facts] "We will not quote a tighter figure before seeing the room." : a Migss quoting policy that is not in the pack. "Any firm that does is guessing" is also an unsupported jab at competitors.
- [facts] "You never reach the end with a surprise figure." : absolute assurance that goes beyond the pack's "no hidden costs" and "amend as it happens".
- [structure] "Bathroom renovation cost in Essex typically sits at [Migss bathroom price range]." : the excerpt sentence is repeated word for word as the opening paragraph.
Best line: "Rushing or skipping a stage hides problems behind finished tiles."

## D2
Verdict: FAIL
Scores: voice 6, usefulness 9, specificity 8
Issues:
- [rule of three] "how much you want to spend, and how much cleaning you are willing to do" : the intro lists three things the answer "depends on" in parallel form.
- [rule of three] "They differ in how the water is contained, how much building work sits underneath the tiles" : parallel "how" phrases, with a third ("how the room behaves day to day") in the same sentence.
- [rule of three] "a single unit that is bedded, levelled and connected" : three parallel participles for rhythm.
- [rule of three] "we will look at your floor, your layout and your budget" : parallel triad in the closing CTA.
- [facts] "we usually suggest pricing both this option and a full wet room before you decide" : a Migss process rule of thumb that is not in the pack. Minor.
- [facts] "Level access is the main reason most people choose one" : sweeping market claim with no source. Minor.
- [voice] "This is why who builds a wet room matters more than which drain or tile you choose." : ungrammatical. "This is why the installer matters more than the drain or tile you choose" would read cleanly.
- [spec] Length is 2,489 words, just under the 2,500 ceiling. FAQ has 5 questions, which is within the limit. Links are all from the allowed list.
Best line: "Timber is usually easier to form a fall in, as long as the joists are sound"

## D3
Verdict: FAIL
Scores: voice 6, usefulness 8, specificity 7
Issues:
- [throat-clearing] "If you have searched for bathroom renovation cost Essex figures, you will have seen a wide spread of prices." : filler opener before any fact. The brief asks for the price range to lead, and here the band first appears in the third paragraph.
- [voice] "A bathroom renovation cost Essex homeowners face depends mainly on the work involved and the finishes chosen." : the excerpt stacks nouns awkwardly ("cost Essex homeowners face"), so it reads as a rough draft.
- [logic] "Pick tiles and taps early, so your budget is set before the design stage begins." : a design stage only applies to full design, supply and build. The same draft calls fit only the typical route, so this advice does not fit that route. Minor.
- [spec] 784 words, within 700 to 900. No FAQ, which is correct. The CTA links to /contact.
Best line: "a change made late usually costs more to put right than one agreed at the start"

## Ranking

Wet room drafts (D2, D4, D5):
1. D2: the most useful to a homeowner, with real floor guidance (timber vs concrete, joist checks, step-free options), but it has four rhythm triads and sits close to the 2,500-word ceiling.
2. D5: the most direct recommendation and the fewest hard breaches (one triad, one "not just"), but thinner on floor detail than D2.
3. D4: solid content, but the generic opener, the invented customer observation and the unsourced process claim make it the least trustworthy of the three.

Cost guides (D1, D3, D6):
1. D6: leads with the price band and has the fewest breaches (one triad, two generic claims), but its CTA has no link and its sections are thinner.
2. D1: the most concrete practical advice, but it has two unverified Migss claims, a triad in the body and another in the CTA, and repeats its excerpt sentence as the opening.
3. D3: a useful checklist, but it opens with throat-clearing, delays the price band and gives advice that does not fit the fit-only route.
