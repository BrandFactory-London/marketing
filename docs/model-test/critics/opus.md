# Critic report: Migss Interiors drafts

Checks run on all six: em dashes (none found in any draft), US spellings (none found), banned phrase scan, excerpt length (all under 160 characters), word count (all within spec), FAQ count, internal link paths (all six use only allowed paths). The spec requirements (word count, FAQ rule, leading with the price range) are treated as hard requirements alongside Tier 1 rules.

## D1
Cost guide. 852 words. Leads with price range. No FAQ. Links: /renovation-services/bathroom, /resources/bathroom, /our-projects, /contact.
Verdict: FAIL
Scores (1 to 10): voice 8, usefulness 8, specificity 8
Issues:
- [rule of three] "A bigger room means more tiling, more materials and more labour hours." : repeated "more X" triplet used for rhythm.
- [rule of three] "we will go through your room, your plans and your budget" : "your X, your Y and your Z" triplet in the CTA.
- [facts discipline, minor] "We will not quote a tighter figure before seeing the room. Any firm that does is guessing." : Migss quoting policy and a swipe at competitors, neither in the pack.
- [facts discipline, minor] "Some competitors subcontract the work without it, and that is where mistakes slip through." : the pack supports the first half only. The causal claim goes beyond it.
- [accuracy, minor] "You never reach the end with a surprise figure." : reads as a guarantee. The pack only says changes are amended as they happen.
- [clarity] "The second route includes a design stage, so the total differs." : vague, and it can mislead because the design fee is deducted from the build invoice.
- [quality] The [Migss bathroom price range] placeholder appears in the excerpt, the intro, the first H2 and the CTA. Once the range is filled in, this will read as repetitive.
Best line: "Better still, show us the product before you pay for it."

## D2

Verdict: FAIL

## Ranking

Overall: all six fail on at least one hard rule. In D1, D2, D4 and D6 the breach is a single rule-of-three triplet that takes one line to fix. D3 and D5 have structural problems that need more rework.

Wet room drafts (comparison):
1. D2: the most concrete and decision-useful draft (tray heights, materials, electrical zones, grout choice, six situation-based verdicts). It needs two triplets fixed, one questionable joist claim checked, and one invented "we usually suggest" practice removed.
2. D4: the most honest voice, with a strong small-room verdict and resale section. It is slightly less technical than D2 and has an invented customer anecdote plus consultation-scope claims.
3. D5: has useful practical tests, but it contains a banned "not just" construction, a muddled cost section, a CTA with no heading, and no local specificity.

Cost drafts:
1. D6: the best real-world examples (soil stack, top-floor flat, freestanding bath blocking the door). It leads with the range and has one triplet to fix. It needs a /contact link in the CTA.
2. D1: well structured, with clear save and do-not-save advice that sounds like Migss. It has two triplets, a competitor swipe and an overused price placeholder.
3. D3: fails the "lead with the price range" spec, opens with throat-clearing and stuffs the keyword in ungrammatically. The quote checklist is good, but the draft needs restructuring.
